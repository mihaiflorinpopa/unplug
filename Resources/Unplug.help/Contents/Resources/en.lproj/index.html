Unplug Help

Unplug makes macOS forget a display, as if you unplugged it, and brings it back with one click. The cable stays in, so the monitor can keep charging your Mac and powering its USB hub while its screen is off.

Turn a display off or on

Click the display icon in the menu bar, then click the display's name. A checkmark means the display is on.

Brightness

Each display that supports it has a brightness control: a slider under its name in the menu, and one in Settings > Displays. It works for the built-in screen, Apple displays and most other monitors. Monitors that don't answer brightness commands don't show one.

Linked brightness

Turn on Link Brightness for two or more displays in Settings > Displays to keep them at the same brightness. The menu then lists them under Linked Displays, with one slider for all of them. A display you link takes the level the others already have. Each linked display still turns off and on by itself. unplug brightness sets linked displays together too. A change made with a monitor's own buttons isn't copied to the others, since Unplug doesn't keep checking monitors.

Keyboard backlight

On a MacBook, choose Keyboard Backlight in the menu to turn the backlight on or off. In Settings > Displays you can also have it go off with the built-in screen and come back with it.

Built-in screen and monitors

Turn on Turn Off When a Monitor Connects in Settings > Displays to switch the built-in screen off whenever you plug in a monitor. Whatever the setting, the built-in screen always comes back on when the last monitor is unplugged.

Keyboard shortcuts

Control-Option-Command-U (⌃⌥⌘U) reconnects every display. Control-Option-Command-B (⌃⌥⌘B) turns the built-in screen off or on. They work in any app. Change them or turn them off in Settings > Shortcuts.

Settings

Choose Settings in the menu, or press Command-Comma (⌘,). Settings opens on the pane you used last.

Displays
Each display's on/off switch, brightness slider and Link Brightness switch, plus Turn Off When a Monitor Connects, Keyboard Backlight and Turn Off Keyboard Backlight with Screen.
Reconnect Displays on Quit
Quitting Unplug turns every display back on.
Launch at Login
Opens Unplug when you log in. If macOS asks you to allow it, turn Unplug on in System Settings > General > Login Items.
Keep Displays Off
Displays you turned off stay off after a restart and when they're plugged back in. It never turns off your last screen.
Show in Menu Bar
Shows Unplug's icon in the menu bar.
Show in Dock
Uses Unplug as a regular app, with a Dock icon and a window.
Check for Updates Automatically
Checks for a new version when Unplug opens and once a day, and asks before installing it. When it finds one, the menu shows Update Available.
Shortcuts
The keyboard shortcuts above. Click one to change it.

Use Unplug from the Dock

Turn on Show in Dock in Settings > General. Click Unplug's Dock icon to open its window, or Control-click the icon to turn a display off or on from the Dock menu. You can then turn off Show in Menu Bar if you don't want the menu bar icon.

After a restart

Logging out or restarting brings every display back. Turn on Keep Displays Off and Unplug turns the displays you left off back off when it opens at login. With it on, a monitor you turned off also stays off when you plug it back in, for example with a dock.

If something goes wrong

Press ⌃⌥⌘U, or quit Unplug: quitting reconnects every display. Logging out or restarting always brings every display back. Unplug won't turn off your last working display. If you unplugged a display while it was off, choose Forget under it in the menu.

Command line

The unplug command is inside the app. To use it in Terminal, link it once:

sudo ln -sf /Applications/Unplug.app/Contents/Helpers/unplug /usr/local/bin/unplug

Then run unplug help to see every command.
